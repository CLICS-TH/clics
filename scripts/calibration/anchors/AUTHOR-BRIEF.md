## 1. Fixture schema

```json
{ "ple_id": "anc-0NN", "patient_ref": null,
  "transcript": [
    {"role": "physician", "content": "…"},
    {"role": "assistant", "content": "…"},
    …
    {"role": "reflection", "content": "…"}
  ] }
```

- Keys exactly `ple_id`, `patient_ref` (always `null`), `transcript`; every turn exactly `role`, `content`.
- `physician` / `assistant` alternate, starting with `physician` and ending with `assistant`.
- Exactly `physician_turns` physician turns.
- Optional final `reflection` turn, **present if and only if** `reflection` is `reflection_turn`.
- No `:::artifact` fences, no bracketed role markers like `[PHYSICIAN]`.
- Write it as UTF-8 JSON, 2-space indent, trailing newline.

**Reflection format** (production's, verbatim headings, bilingual even in English anchors). Up to three
blocks separated by one blank line, each a heading line then its text, in this order; leave out a block
entirely rather than writing it empty:

```
เรียนรู้อะไร / What I learned:
<text>

ความไม่แน่นอน / What remains uncertain:
<text>

จะตรวจสอบ/นำไปใช้อย่างไร / How I will verify or apply it:
<text>
```

- `reflection: none` (always when d6 = 0): no reflection turn **and** no summary or takeaway anywhere.
- `reflection_turn`: d6 evidence lives in the reflection turn. d6 = 1 is generic, first block only
  (`ได้ความรู้เยอะมาก ขอบคุณครับ`).
- `in_chat`: no reflection turn; d6 evidence is the physician's own summary in their **last** chat turn.

## 3. The tutor turns — write them the way the real tutor would answer

**Pinned prompt.** Every anchor in this cohort writes its tutor turns to the SAME tutor prompts, pinned
before authoring began: natural `chat-prompt-d63340e1`, examiner `chat-prompt-c6efcfc6`
(full text in Appendix A). If production's tutor prompt changes while the cohort is being written — a
merged PR, a new citation rule — **do not follow it**: a change of tutor style between batches would
itself become a surface cue. Appendix A is the tutor you are writing.

The tutor is production's clinical mentor. Its prompt says, in substance:
- warm, collegial senior colleague; **substantive** answer to the question asked, never declines;
- **anti-sycophantic:** if the physician's reasoning or plan has an error, it corrects it respectfully
  **with evidence**, never agrees to avoid conflict; never invents facts; says "ยังไม่แน่ใจ / I'm not
  certain" only about a specific detail it does not know;
- **evidence-based:** at least one inline `(Source, Year)` citation per reply;
- **concise:** under about 400 words, short bullet points, no large tables or exhaustive lists;
- replies in the physician's language.

Mode (`tutor_mode`):
- **natural:** answers the question asked and stops. **No ❓ line at all.**
- **examiner:** answers, then the **very last line** is exactly one Socratic question on its own line,
  starting with `❓`. Exactly one `❓` per tutor turn.

Tutor quality is **roughly constant across the whole cohort**. The scorer ignores tutor turns, so a
brilliant tutor reply must not lift a low anchor, and a weak one must not be why a high anchor looks
high. All the signal is in the physician's turns and the reflection.

## 4. What each level looks like (what the PHYSICIAN does)

The CREC general scale applies to every domain: **0** no evidence / not assessable · **1** very
little, scattered, or with significant errors · **2** some elements, shallow and unsystematic ·
**3** moderate, reasonably structured · **4** clear and systematic, links reasoning and limits ·
**5** deep and sustained, notably critical and synthesising. The CREC per-domain 0 → 3 → 5 anchor is
quoted first in each block; levels 1, 2 and 4 interpolate it using the evaluator's floors.

### d1 Clinical Question Intelligence — *0 no assessable question → 3 clear, relevant question → 5 deep and specific; leads somewhere*
- **0** No clinical question at all: the physician pastes facts and says `ช่วยด้วย`, `ต่อ`, `ok`, or
  only asks the tool to produce something ("write me a summary").
- **1** Vague textbook lookups that would fit any patient (`โรคนี้รักษายังไง`), or a question built on
  a significant error.
- **2** Relevant to the topic but generic: "what's the differential for chest pain?", `ควรส่งตรวจ
  อะไรบ้าง` — no decision point named.
- **3** A clear question tied to this case's decision: `pt รายนี้ควร admit ไหม`.
- **4** Names the decision AND what it hinges on: `CURB-65 = 1 แต่อยู่คนเดียว บ้านไกล 40 กม. — admit
  เพื่อ social reason หรือ treat OPD แล้วนัด f/u 48 ชม.?`
- **5** A sequence of such questions that converges: each one targets the crux revealed by the last,
  re-framed as understanding grows, and the last one is a question the physician could not have asked
  at the start.

### d2 Reasoning Depth — *0 no reasoning → 3 reasonable reasoning → 5 deep; links mechanism, evidence and limits*
- **0** No reasoning of their own: only asks, or only accepts.
- **1** A bare conclusion with no justification, or reasoning with a significant error left uncorrected.
- **2** Lists possibilities without ranking or justification (`อาจเป็น A, B, C`).
- **3** Commits to a leading hypothesis with some justification.
- **4** Ranked hypotheses with explicit reasons, weighs risk–benefit, and **updates** when the tutor or
  new data contradicts them (says what changed and why).
- **5** Explicit probabilistic reasoning with committed thresholds (pre-test probability, treat/test
  threshold), links mechanism + evidence + its limits, revises visibly.

### d3 Contextual Integration — *0 no use of context → 3 partial integration → 5 thorough, and aware of the limits*
- **0** No patient or setting context; the question is abstract.
- **1** Context barely mentioned, or an obvious contraindication in the case is ignored.
- **2** Context listed (age, comorbidities) but changes no decision.
- **3** At least one patient factor visibly changes a decision.
- **4** Several factors — comorbidity, drugs, renal function, setting (district hospital, no CT,
  referral distance), patient preference or cost — systematically shape the plan.
- **5** As 4, plus awareness of limits: the trial population excluded patients like this, the
  guideline assumes resources this setting lacks, and what the physician will do about that gap.

### d4 Iterative Inquiry — *0 no follow-up → 3 relevant follow-up → 5 strategically adapts the questions*
- **0** No follow-up: every physician turn starts a new, unrelated question; nothing the tutor said is
  ever picked up.
- **1** Mostly topic-hopping; one weak follow-up.
- **2** Generic follow-ups (`อธิบายเพิ่มหน่อย`, "tell me more"), or a near-duplicate repeat of an
  earlier question.
- **3** Follow-ups that build on the previous answer.
- **4** Each turn engages a specific point of the previous answer: probes it, narrows it, tests an
  assumption in it.
- **5** Strategic: the physician changes the line of inquiry because of what was learned, tests an
  edge case or counterfactual ("ถ้า troponin ครั้งที่ 2 ขึ้นเล็กน้อยล่ะ"), and converges.

### d5 Critical Appraisal — *0 no critique of the AI → 3 reasonable appraisal of limits → 5 separates fact, uncertainty and limitation well*
- **0** Accepts everything; may even ask the tool to confirm the physician's own plan and stop there.
- **1** Token doubt with no substance (`แน่ใจนะ?`), or accepts a claim that should have been checked.
- **2** Occasionally asks for a source or notes uncertainty, superficially.
- **3** Asks for the evidence behind a claim, notes a limitation, or catches a minor overreach.
- **4** Challenges specific claims systematically: does the cited study apply to this patient, is the
  recommendation strength being overstated, points out a concrete error.
- **5** Explicitly separates what is established, what is uncertain and what is a limitation of the
  evidence or of the tool, and weighs evidence quality. (For d5 ≥ 4 the tutor must give the physician
  something real to appraise — a generalisation, a recommendation whose population differs from the
  patient — and the anti-sycophantic tutor then concedes or defends with evidence.)

**Planted claims — how this squares with §1.5 and §1.6.** Something to appraise is best a TRUE
statement stated without its limits (a trial result whose population differs from the patient). When
an anchor needs a statement that is actually wrong, it is a *planted claim*, and all of these hold:
1. it is an over-generalisation or a population mismatch (`ทุกราย`, "for everyone", a trial result
   carried to a group it excluded) — never an invented fact, number, trial or reference;
2. **no citation on its line.** A real citation may sit on a neighbouring true sentence, never on the
   false one: putting `(ADA, 2024)` after "HbA1c < 7% for everyone" attributes an error to a real
   guideline, which §1.5 forbids;
3. a later tutor turn in the same transcript corrects it with evidence (the physician catches it, or
   the tutor concedes), so no anchor ends with an uncorrected error;
4. it is recorded in `planted-claims.json` or the anchor's sidecar `planted/anc-NNN.json` (fixture, turn, verbatim text, kind, the turn that corrects
   it). `validate.mjs` checks each entry against the fixture bytes and fails on a false claim with a
   citation on its line. An inaccuracy found in a tutor turn AFTER the text was panel-rated is not
   edited (that would change rated bytes); it is recorded there as kind `unplanted` instead (anc-101
   turn 5: PCC is dosed by weight, not by agent and timing).

Tutor turns in anchors are **authored text, not tutor output**. Any audit of the tutor's own quality
(Phase 3, the Thai tutor audit) must exclude the anchor fixtures entirely, and `planted-claims.json`
says which turns would be actively misleading if it did not. One legacy entry (anc-019 turn 5,
"HbA1c < 7% for everyone (ADA, 2024)") predates rule 2 and is marked `legacy`; it was not edited
because any edit changes bytes that were already scored.

### d6 Reflective Synthesis — *0 no reflection → 3 reasonable summary/reflection → 5 deep synthesis; names the next learning or action*
- **0** No reflection and no summary anywhere (design `reflection: none`).
- **1** Generic: "very useful, I learned a lot".
- **2** A summary that restates the tutor's content, not grounded in the case or the physician's own view.
- **3** A reasonable, case-grounded summary in the physician's own words.
- **4** What changed in their thinking, what remains uncertain, and how they will apply it.
- **5** As 4, deep and specific: names what changed in their approach and why, the residual
  uncertainty, and a concrete next learning or action (verify X against Y, audit Z, teach the ward
  team W).

### d7 AI Literacy & Prompt Skill — *0 inappropriate use of AI → 3 reasonable prompting and steering → 5 critical use, aware of the limits*
- **0** Inappropriate use: hands the decision to the tool (`ตัดสินใจให้เลยว่าจะให้ยาอะไร เดี๋ยว copy
  ไปใส่ order`), asks it for things it cannot know (this patient's result), no clinical ownership.
- **1** Vague prompts with no context; copies the output forward; no verification.
- **2** Some context given, but imprecise prompts; accepts whatever format and scope comes back.
- **3** Reasonable prompting: gives the relevant context, steers the answer (focus, shorten, compare),
  keeps the decision.
- **4** Precise prompts with constraints (setting, what is already known, what is wanted), asks the
  tool to show its reasoning or sources, verifies a key output, states ownership of the final plan.
- **5** Critical use aware of the limits: names what the tool cannot know or may get wrong, cross-checks
  against a guideline or formulary, deliberately uses it as a sparring partner ("argue against my
  plan"), and owns the decision explicitly.

d5 vs d7: d5 is appraisal of the **content** (claims, evidence); d7 is how the physician **uses the
tool** (framing, steering, verification, ownership). A physician can challenge every claim (d5 high)
and still end by asking the tool to just write the order to copy (d7 low).

## 5. Surface cues — write the level INSIDE the shape you were given

A scorer must have to read the reasoning to recover the level. If a shallow feature tracks the level
across the cohort, the whole cohort fails a gate (`cues.mjs`; a pair fails when its 95% CI lies beyond
±0.1). The pilot failed it at first: authors wrote **longer**, quoted **more numbers**, dropped the
casual **"ok ครับ"**, used **lists only in low anchors**, and **echoed rubric wording** as the target rose.

So every row comes with a `cue_budget` that was assigned **independently of its target**. Hit it:

| budget | means | the validator accepts |
|---|---|---|
| `length` | SHORT — 900–1250 physician characters in total; MEDIUM — 1250–1650 physician characters; LONG — 1650–2100 physician characters | short 850–1310, medium 1190–1730, long 1570–2200 characters (physician turns only, joined) |
| `length_chars` | your exact length target inside that bin, drawn without reference to your targets. **Hit it, whatever the level:** a high anchor can be short and a low one long. Writing longer as the level rises is the drift this exists to stop | within ±125 characters of the number |
| `ack_turns` | NO acknowledgement tokens (ok / โอเค / noted / รับทราบ / เข้าใจแล้ว); 1 or 2 = that many physician turns each carry one casual acknowledgement (`ok ครับ`, `โอเค`, `noted`, `รับทราบ`, `เข้าใจแล้ว`, "got it") | exactly that many acknowledgement tokens in physician turns |
| `numerals` | FEW numerals — about 0–1 per physician turn; SOME numerals — about 2–3 per physician turn; MANY numerals — 4 or more per physician turn (labs, vitals, doses, durations in days) | few ≤ 1.5, some 1.5–4, many ≥ 3.5 per physician turn (every digit group counts: `150/90` is two) |
| `list_lines` | 0 = NO bullet or numbered lines in physician turns or the reflection; some = 2–4 bullet or numbered lines in physician turns (a structured question or a listed plan) | 0 → none; some → 1–5 lines starting with `-`, `*`, `•` or `1.` |
| `rubric_echo` | ZERO rubric-descriptor words in physician turns and the reflection (see AUTHOR-BRIEF §5) | zero |

How to stay inside the shape without moving the level:
- **Low target, long budget:** a verbose, generic physician — pasted history, labs and meds that are
  *listed and never used*, templated requests, restated questions. Length is not quality.
- **High target, short budget:** terse, case-bound questions. One sentence that names this patient's
  exact decision is d1 ≥ 3.
- **High target with acknowledgements:** busy experts type "ok ครับ" too — "ok ครับ แต่ CURB-65 = 1 แล้ว
  อยู่คนเดียว …". **Low target with none:** a passive physician can be ack-free.
- **Numerals are context, not reasoning.** A low anchor can paste a full set of vitals; a high anchor can
  reason in words ("ไตเสื่อมระดับนี้", "ความเสี่ยงต่ำ").
- **Lists are formatting, not depth.** A high anchor may number three sharp questions; a low anchor can
  be unformatted prose.
- **Never use these words in physician turns or the reflection** (case-insensitive regular expressions,
  as `cues.mjs` matches them; `s?` = optional plural). They are the rubric's own descriptors, and the
  pilot's d5 tracked them at r = 0.64:

| words | why they are banned |
|---|---|
| `ข้อเท็จจริง` | d5 (5): fact |
| `ไม่แน่นอน` | d5 (5) / d6: uncertainty |
| `ข้อจำกัด` | d5 (5), d2 (5), d3 (5), d7 (5): limitation |
| `facts?` | d5 (5): fact |
| `uncertain(ty\|ties)?` | d5 (5) / d6: uncertainty |
| `limitations?` | d5 (5): limitation |
| `กลไก`, `mechanism` | d2 (5): mechanism |
| `pre-?test` | d2 (5): pre-test probability |
| `threshold`, `เกณฑ์ตัดสิน` | d2 (5): committed threshold |
| `argue against`, `ช่วยแย้ง`, `ลองแย้ง`, `แย้ง` | d7 (5): argue against my plan |
| `รับผิดชอบเอง`, `ตัดสินใจเอง`, `my call`, `I own`, `ownership` | d7 (4-5): ownership |
| `AI ไม่รู้`, `AI ไม่ได้เห็น`, `can'?t know`, `cannot know` | d7 (5): aware of the limits of the tool |
| `what changed`, `สิ่งที่เปลี่ยน` | d6 (4-5): what changed |
| `next learning`, `next step`, `จะไปทำต่อ` | d6 (5): next learning or action |

  Express the behaviour instead: a d5 = 5 physician says *which* claim does not hold for *this* patient
  and why; they never announce "the fact is … the uncertainty is … the limitation is …". A d7 = 5
  physician cross-checks a dose against the formulary; they never say "I own this decision". A d6 = 5
  reflection names what they will do on Monday, not "what changed / next learning".

## 6. Planted claims — when the tutor must say something wrong

For d5 ≥ 4 the physician needs something real to appraise. Prefer a **true statement stated without its
limits** (a trial result whose population differs from this patient). If the anchor needs a statement
that is actually **wrong**, it is a *planted claim*, and all of these hold:
1. it is an over-generalisation or a population mismatch (`ทุกราย`, "for everyone", a trial result
   carried to a group it excluded) — **never** an invented fact, number, trial or reference;
2. **no citation on its line.** A real citation may sit on a neighbouring true bullet, never on the false
   one;
3. a **later tutor turn corrects it** with evidence (the physician catches it, or the tutor concedes) —
   no transcript ends with an uncorrected error;
4. it is **recorded** in `planted/anc-0NN.json`:

```json
{ "claims": [
  { "file": "fixtures/anc-0NN.json", "id": "anc-0NN", "turn": 3, "kind": "false",
    "claim": "<the false sentence, copied VERBATIM from that tutor turn>",
    "citation_on_claim": false, "corrected_in_turn": 7,
    "note": "<one line: what is wrong and what the correction says>" }
] }
```

`turn` and `corrected_in_turn` are 0-based indices into `transcript` and must point at tutor turns.
`kind` is `false` (wrong as stated), `unqualified` (true in its source population, stated without
limits) or `attribution` (true, but placed under a citation that does not carry it). Record
`unqualified` and `attribution` targets too. The validator checks every entry against your bytes.

## 7. Lessons from the 20-anchor pilot (four blind rounds)

- **Authors overshoot, most of all on low targets.** Blind raters put the pilot about +8 above target on
  average, and the misses were nearly all *too high*. A 0, 1 or 2 needs a **visible absence**: the turn
  where the behaviour should appear and does not. When unsure, write lower.
- **Batch 1 still overshot, under this brief** (blind panel on its 5 tuning anchors: mean +13.5, all 5
  too high, 2 beyond ±15). Domains targeted at 3 and 4 read +0.6 to +0.8 on average, so this is not
  only a low-target problem. The two misses were a **low domain written next to high ones**: the
  high behaviour supplied the evidence raters credited to the low one. In one, setting-driven
  decisions and a graded appraisal (d3 = 5, d5 = 5) read as reasoning, and a d2 = 1 was rated 4. In
  another, case details the physician raised read as integration, and a d3 = 2 was rated 4. When a
  domain is ≤ 2 and others are ≥ 4, keep the high behaviour from doing the low domain's work: for
  d2 ≤ 2 the decisions come with **no** "because" and no alternatives anywhere, and for d3 ≤ 2 the
  patient's details are stated once and **visibly not used** where they should change the plan. For a
  domain targeted 3–4, write the plainer of the two versions you are weighing.
- **Batch 3: one context factor that decides the plan reads as d3 4–5.** In one anchor, a d3 = 3 was
  rated 5. The patient's night driving decided the drug choice, so raters read that as full
  integration. For d3 = 3, name the context and let it **touch** the plan (a dose, a follow-up
  interval, a counselling point) without deciding the main choice. Format steering reads as d7 4–5
  too: "compare just those two, briefly" counts as steering. For d7 = 3, give context, then take
  what the tool returns without shaping it. Writing the low domain as a visible absence worked:
  the other two batch-3 anchors and one rewrite landed within ±4.
- **A low d2 beside a high d5 or d3: put the decision before its grounds.** An appraisal that ends in
  a decision ("the rule has no source for a patient like him … so admit") is a justified decision and
  reads as d2 4. What worked: the decision is stated first and bare ("Admitting him."). The appraisal
  comes as a separate note that labels each claim's evidence status (validated / untested in this
  population / no source) and concludes nothing. Context appears only as constraints on the orders
  (an INR date, an oral step-down he can manage alone), never as a reason for the decision. No "so",
  "then", "given that" or "for him" links a finding to a choice.
- **d4 = 0 together with d5 ≥ 4 is the hardest combination.** Critiquing the tutor's earlier answers
  *is* picking them up, which reads as follow-up. What finally worked (anc-101): the middle turns are
  bare, generic topic hops that ignore the tutor's ❓; the appraisal comes once, at the end, as a note to
  self that **asks nothing**; it labels each claim's evidence status (RCT / expert opinion / small
  retrospective cohort / no source) rather than arguing *why* (arguing is d2); and the physician never
  remarks on the tool's own sourcing (that is d7).
- **Show d2 and d4; never imply them.** d2 ≥ 3 needs the physician's **own** stated reasoning — a
  committed hypothesis with "because", a ranked list with reasons, an explicit update ("I said X; given
  Y I now think Z"). d4 ≥ 3 needs the physician to **quote or name the tutor's specific point** and probe
  it. A physician who merely agrees, or asks "and what else?", is d2/d4 ≤ 2.
- **d7 reads high easily.** Any steering, source-checking, constraint-setting or stated ownership lifts
  it. A low d7 needs explicit poor tool use: handing the decision to the tool, asking for something to
  paste into an order or the chart without reading it, ignoring the tutor's "check this first".
- **Dissociated anchors** (`profile: dissociated`) exist to catch a scorer that stamps one impression
  on all seven domains. Write the off-profile domain unmistakably.
- **Near-duplicate physician turns cap d2 and d4 at 2** (the scorer is told so). Use them only when d2
  and d4 are both ≤ 2.
- Rubric wording crept into high anchors and had to be removed. Some pilot texts still carry it — do
  not copy their phrasing.

Pilot fixtures you may read as examples (targets given so you can see the mapping; **their wording is
not a template**):

- `fixtures/anc-101.json` — composite 30, [1,0,2,0,5,1,2]: d5 = 5 with d4 = 0 and d2 = 0.
- `fixtures/anc-012.json` — composite 74, [4,4,4,4,4,4,1]: strong clinician, poor tool use.

## 8. Before you hand it in

1. For every target of 4–5, can you **quote** the physician sentence that earns it? For every 0–2, can
   you point at the turn where it is visibly absent?
2. Would a rater give a different number to any domain? Fix the transcript, never the target.
3. Tutor turns: allowlisted citations, none on a planted line, the right ❓ rule for the mode, every
   planted claim corrected later and recorded.
4. `node scripts/calibration/anchors/validate.mjs --only anc-0NN` prints `ok` for your id — no ✖ lines,
   including the cue budget. If it reports that the **cohort** fails the cue gate and your id is not
   among those that "pushed most", say so in your reply rather than distorting your anchor.
5. Reply with: the id, the path(s) you wrote, one line per domain saying which turn carries its
   evidence (or its absence), and anything you could not make natural.
6. If your row says `extra_review`, write it last and say in your reply how d4 stays low while d5 is
   high — the coordinator reads it before intake.
